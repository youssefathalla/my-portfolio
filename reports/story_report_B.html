### Step 4: The Story

Fifteen years of Arthur Vance could be summarized in 12-point Calibri font: gray, aligned, and strictly formatted to avoid conflict. As senior accountant at Apex Logistics, Arthur’s greatest thrill was a balanced reconciliation. 

Until the Tuesday Derek cornered him by the industrial coffee machine.

"I don't care about your columns, Arthur," Derek snarled, trapping Arthur against the Formica counter with a thick forearm. The regional VP loomed like a human avalanche, demanding falsified expense reports to cover his own audit trail. "You make the numbers fit, or I see to it your desk is in the basement by noon. Understand?"

Arthur’s heart hammered a frantic, irregular beat against his ribs. Every survival instinct screamed *retreat*. He opened his mouth to whisper his usual surrender—*“Yes, Mr. Henderson, right away”*—but his vocal cords violently rejected the cowardice. 

Instead of a squeak, a seismic vibration tore upward from Arthur’s diaphragm. 

He didn't speak. He *released*.

An apocalyptic, pitch-perfect High C erupted from Arthur’s throat. It wasn't human. It was a cathedral-shattering, glass-rattling operatic resonance born of pure, distilled terror. 

The breakroom microwave’s digital clock shattered. The fluorescent bulb overhead flickered, buzzed, and violently imploded, raining tiny white sparks over the linoleum. The shockwave of sound rattled the ceramic mugs in the cabinets. 

Derek staggered backward, clutching his ears, his face draining of all corporate bravado. His mouth hung open in a mute, trembling "O." 

Arthur slammed his mouth shut. 

Silence rushed back into the room, heavy and trembling. Arthur dropped his head, clutching his throat. His vocal cords felt like they had been scrubbed with coarse-grit sandpaper, and a hot, metallic taste of copper flooded his mouth. His lungs burned as if he’d inhaled fire, and his knees shook so violently he had to grip the counter to stay upright.

Derek stared at him, wide-eyed, chest heaving. The aggressive VP didn't demand altered ledgers. He didn't threaten the basement. He simply backed away slowly, whispering, "Jesus Christ, man," before fleeing down the hall.

Arthur stood alone in the dark, smoking breakroom. He swallowed hard, wincing at the razor-blade pain in his throat, and adjusted his glasses. 

*Note to self,* Arthur thought, his hands finally steadying. *Add 'Acoustic Weaponry' to the personal development portfolio.*
